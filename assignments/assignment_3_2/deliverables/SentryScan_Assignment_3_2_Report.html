1. Executive Summary & Theoretical Context

Assignment 3.2 equips SentryScan with a formal Multi-Attribute Utility Theory (MAUT) optimization engine (Keeney & Raiffa, 1976; Russell & Norvig, Ch. 16), enabling rational decision-making across competing operational objectives.

1.1 Rational Agency & Utility Theory

A rational agent acts to maximize expected utility. In cyber vulnerability management, plans must balance five competing dimensions: 1. Severity Coverage ($C_{sev}$): Fraction of detected critical vulnerabilities addressed (benefit). 2. Host Coverage ($C_{hosts}$): Fraction of network fleet endpoints audited (benefit). 3. Execution Cost ($Cost$): Computational / bandwidth resource consumption (cost). 4. Completion Time ($Time$): Wall-clock duration in milliseconds (cost). 5. Residual Risk ($Risk$): Unmitigated exposure likelihood (cost).

2. Architecture & Implementation Specification

2.1 Normalized Multi-Attribute Utility Formula

For each candidate plan $o$, attributes are normalized to $[0, 1]$ across the candidate pool: $$U(o) = w_1\bar{C}{sev} + w_2\bar{C}{hosts} - w_3\overline{Cost} - w_4\overline{Time} - w_5\overline{Risk}$$ where: $$\sum_{i=1}^5 w_i = 1.0, \quad w_i \ge 0$$

2.2 Infeasibility Filtering & Hard Constraints

Before utility computation, candidates exceeding operational thresholds are strictly filtered: - $\text{Cost} > \text{Budget}{max} \implies U(o) = -\infty$ (Rejection: COST_EXCEEDED) - $\text{Time} > \text{Deadline}{max} \implies U(o) = -\infty$ (Rejection: TIME_EXCEEDED) - $\text{BatchList} = \emptyset \implies U(o) = -\infty$ (Rejection: EMPTY_BATCH_LIST)

The rational choice is the feasible argmax: $$o^* = \arg\max_{o \in O_{feas}} U(o)$$ Ties are resolved deterministically by comparing $(C_{sev}, C_{hosts}, -Cost, \text{plan_id})$.
