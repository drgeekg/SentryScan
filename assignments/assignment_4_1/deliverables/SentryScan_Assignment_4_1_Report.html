1. Executive Summary & Theoretical Context

Assignment 4.1 implements the Belief–Desire–Intention (BDI) cognitive architecture (Bratman, 1987; Rao & Georgeff, 1995; Wooldridge, Ch. 4) for SentryScan's Analyst Agent.

1.1 The Classical BDI Paradigm

A pure reflex agent reacts instantly without commitment to long-term goals. A BDI agent deliberates and commits to intentions: - Beliefs ($B$): The agent's knowledge about the operational environment (host states, open ports, observed vulnerabilities, active policies). - Desires ($D$): The potential goals or operational states the agent wishes to achieve (audit fleet, contain compromised hosts, surface critical alerts). - Intentions ($I$): The committed desires chosen for execution, organized into concrete procedural plans.

1.2 Intention Reconsideration & Epistemic Preemption

An agent cannot re-deliberate after every minor percept (computational waste), nor can it pursue a plan stubbornly when the environment has changed catastrophically: - Kinny & Georgeff (1991) Reconsideration Trade-off: SentryScan implements bold-agent deliberation with explicit material belief update filters. - Deliberative Preemption: Routine telemetry (e.g., informational banner) retains active plans. The arrival of a CRITICAL vulnerability finding triggers an immediate interrupt, cleanly abandoning obsolete intentions and adopting emergency containment.
